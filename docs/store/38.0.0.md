## What's New
Glyphbox is now Icelus, named for the Greek god who makes dreams look real. Same app, same projects, a new name.

Masks. Crop any part from its edges, or clip it to the shape of another part, and only that much shows. Nothing is cut: clear the crop or the clip and every character comes back.

A real canvas. Buttons, fields, checkboxes, switches, menus, tables and cards now look like the real thing, on screens laid out like artboards. Arrows, paint and charts keep their character style, the text look is one switch away in Settings, and every export works as before.

Adjustment layers. Drag a box with Adjust and everything under it changes without being touched: invert it, dim it, turn it into plain ASCII, or swap every box to double, rounded or heavy lines. Change your mind any time, or hide the layer to see the original.

Color. Give any part an ink and a paper from the 16 terminal colors, and paint strokes in color cell by cell. Copied text stays plain, Copy as ANSI keeps the colors for a terminal, and images and HTML keep them too.

Gradients. Drag to lay a ramp of shade, light to solid, straight across or round like a glow. Shades now fill their whole cell, so ramps, shaded photos and fills read as smooth tone instead of stripes.

Selections. Drag a box around any cells, or tap a mark with the magic wand to pick its whole run. Copy them as text, cut them, fill them, or drag to move what they hold. Whole parts move with it and a box is never cut open.

Brush. Paint any mark freehand, one to five cells wide: a full block, a shade, a dot or a hash. Each stroke is its own layer you can move or delete, and the eraser takes paint away without ever cutting a box open.

Built for agents. Connect Claude or any MCP client to Icelus and it can read worked examples, sketch a screen from a sentence, review it, and build from it.

Present. Export, then Present, and your screens play one at a time, full screen, in reading order. Tap or use the arrow keys to move, and Save as PDF to send the whole deck.

Code that compiles. Export, then Copy React code or Copy SwiftUI code, and your layout becomes a React component with plain CSS or a SwiftUI view: real buttons, fields, checkboxes, tables and headings in the same places. Paste it into your project and restyle from there.

Hand it to Claude Code. Export, then Copy for Claude Code, and you get one build brief: every screen drawn, its parts with their words, place and state, the flow between screens and your notes. Paste it into an agent and it builds to match.

Variations. Ask for variations on any screen and get three takes side by side, stacked, split and dense, from the same parts. Keep the one you like and the others clear away.

Accounts, if you want one. Sign in with email to keep your name with your work, or skip it: everything works without an account. Settings holds the tutorial and the switch for all controls. You can delete your account any time from Profile.

Capture a page. Type a web address and Icelus draws that page as a wireframe: headings, links, buttons, fields, lists and tables, ready to edit.

Easier to start. A short tutorial walks you through your first screen: pick a button, place it, change its words, move it, copy it. The basics sit up front and everything else waits under More.

Design systems. Paste a design system file made from your code and its components join your palette, drawn in text. Review then checks every box against its style.

Review. Tap Review and Icelus checks your screens: parts that overlap, boxes cut open, placeholder labels like Label or Click here, a list with no empty state, a form with no error state. Tap an issue to jump to the part.

Also new: photos in braille or soft shade; pressed, disabled and error states drawn into any part; symbols you change once to change every copy; layers you can hide and lock; groups that move as one; and file trees, sequence diagrams and flowcharts drawn from a few typed lines.

## Description
Icelus is a design tool for screens. Design on a real canvas; underneath, every screen is text.

Pick a part, tap the canvas, and it lands there as a real button, field, switch or table. One switch in Settings shows the same screen as box-drawing characters. Buttons, inputs, tables, date pickers, tab bars, charts, 38 parts in all. Draw arrows between them, add a big title, and move things around until the screen looks right. Or type the whole screen in a few lines and let Icelus draw it.

Then copy it. A Icelus screen is text underneath, so it pastes into a pull request, an issue, a README, a chat or a code comment, and it stays editable wherever it lands. No image to attach, no design file to open.

What you get:
- A real canvas: parts draw as real controls on artboards, with a Settings switch for the text look
- Brush, eraser, selections, gradients, 16 terminal colors, adjustment layers and masks
- 38 parts, each its own object you can move, resize, duplicate and reorder
- Several screens on one canvas, linked by arrows, copied as one document
- Screen templates: sign in, settings, list, detail, empty state
- Highlights, your own saved parts, and iOS, Material, terminal and email packs
- Copy HTML: the layout as real elements for a developer
- Paste any text wireframe and edit it
- Shortcuts: Draw a screen, Make plain ASCII
- 27 languages and full VoiceOver support
- Arrows that stay attached when parts move
- Type a screen in a few lines, and copy any sketch back as those lines
- Turn a photo into block art, or trace over one
- Text boxes, banners and five box styles
- Tables you edit as typed rows, with columns that grow
- Bar charts, sparklines and progress rows drawn from numbers
- Flowcharts from a line like a -> b -> c
- Sequence diagrams from lines like User -> App: Sign in
- Trees from an indented list
- Groups that move as one
- A layer list: hide, lock, reorder
- Symbols: edit one copy, every copy follows
- Pressed, disabled and error states
- Snap and alignment guides
- Copy as text, Markdown or plain ASCII, or share an image
- Autosave and 50 steps of undo
- Keyboard control on the Mac
- The same project file on iPhone, iPad, Mac, Apple Watch and the web

Everything stays on your device. An account is optional, and nothing you draw is uploaded. No tracking.

One price, no subscription. The web version at icelus.jaybulb.com is free.
